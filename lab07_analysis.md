# Lab 07: Comparable-Company Policy and Implied Range

## P/E and its role in valuation

The price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio measures the market price paid for one dollar of earnings: price per share divided by diluted earnings per share (EPS). Price per share is the market value of one common share. Diluted EPS is earnings available to common shareholders divided by the diluted share count, which reflects potentially dilutive securities when applicable.

P/E is useful because it puts companies of different absolute sizes on a per-dollar-of-earnings basis. A smaller company and a larger company can therefore be compared without their share prices or total earnings alone driving the comparison. In a valuation, a peer P/E provides a market-based cross-check to a discounted cash flow (DCF) estimate: a DCF is based on the company's projected cash flows and assumptions, while P/E reflects how the market currently prices comparable earnings.

A lower P/E does not automatically make a company a better investment. It may reflect lower expected growth, higher risk, weaker profitability, more leverage, or a concern that current earnings will not persist. P/E can also mislead when earnings are negative, unusually high or low because of nonrecurring items, or affected by different accounting or capital structures. Differences in growth prospects, business risk, and business models can make two companies' P/E ratios poor substitutes even when they share an industry label.

## Peer policy for Asbury Automotive (ABG)

The peer screen should focus on operating comparability rather than the broad automotive label. Asbury is a franchised vehicle retailer with earnings supported by new- and used-vehicle sales, financing-related activities, and recurring service and parts operations.

| Company | Policy decision | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AutoNation (AN) | Use | AN is a close operating peer because it is also a franchised vehicle retailer with vehicle sales and service/parts operations. Its similar dealership-based model makes its P/E a direct reference point for ABG. |
| Group 1 Automotive (GPI) | Qualify | GPI also operates franchised dealerships and service/parts businesses, so it is relevant. However, differences in scale, geographic footprint, and business mix can affect its growth and risk profile. Its multiple should be included with qualification rather than treated as perfectly interchangeable with ABG. |

Neither company should be excluded solely because it differs from ABG in some respects. The important policy question is whether the core franchised-retail and service/parts economics are sufficiently similar; for AN and GPI, they are, subject to the qualification noted for GPI.

## Validated peer P/E results

| Measure | Result |
| --- | ---: |
| AutoNation P/E | 10.037825x |
| Group 1 Automotive P/E | 11.450149x |
| Peer median P/E | 10.743987x |
| Asbury peer-implied range | $215.81-$246.18 |
| Asbury median-implied price | $231.00 |
| Remove GPI | $215.81 |
| Change from the full-peer median estimate | -$15.18 |

Removing GPI lowers the implied price because GPI has the higher P/E multiple. With both peers included, the median multiple incorporates GPI's 11.450149x P/E. Once GPI is removed, AN's lower 10.037825x P/E is the only multiple applied to ABG's diluted EPS, producing the lower $215.81 estimate.

With only AN remaining, the result is a reference estimate rather than a range. A range needs more than one peer multiple to establish low and high comparison points; one remaining multiple provides only one implied price.

Finally, this comparable-company analysis does not prove that ABG is fairly valued. It is a market-based estimate that depends on peer selection and on the assumption that the peers' earnings multiples are relevant to ABG. Differences in earnings quality, expected growth, risk, and operating mix may justify a different P/E for ABG.
